## Aims

My background and degree is in pharmaceutical chemistry, with most of my practical experience on the experimental side. This is my process in beginning to build  computational skills specifically working toward cheminformatics and ML projects that will be relevant to my optional Python module and hopefully my final year project at the University of Reading. This blog post documents some resources I've been working through.

---

## Charles Weiss Scientific Computing for Chemists with Python

Free online course, good resource for novice beginners, it explains content well written as if it was a notebook as well as having useful exercises at the end of each chapter, covers a lot of areas including fundamental python for scientific use cases. Provides external reading as well. Really good resource as a starting off point

---

## Reaching out to my lecturer

At the university of Reading a new optional module Python, AI and Machine Learning for the Chemical Sciences was made available for 2025/26; with this there is also a computational chemistry focused project available for final year students with Dr Mauricio Cafiero. I had a call to explain my desire to upskill and my interest in project ideas; we went through resources I had found as well as what he recommended, helped me visualise and focus on relevant materials as well as got access to university content on blackboard to ensure I was well prepared for coming back to university after my placement year.

## The Hundred-page Machine Learning Book - Andriy Burkov

Recommend by Dr Cafiero after our talk, available for free on pdf as a 'The book is distributed on the "read first, buy later" principle.' background content on algorithms used in machine learning

## Dr Cafiero's Teaching Repository — CafChemTeach

Dr Cafiero's [CafChemTeach](https://github.com/MauricioCafiero/CafChemTeach) repository contains the notebooks and supporting materials for the Python, Machine Learning and AI for Chemistry module at the University of Reading. It covers building multilayer perceptrons with PyTorch and creating chatbots using Hugging Face models, content that maps directly onto what the module will cover when I return to university.

## Pat Walters Chemoinformatics

https://github.com/PatWalters/practical_cheminformatics_tutorials

A comprehensive collection of Jupyter notebooks covering cheminformatics from the ground up — see the [full overview on DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/PatWalters/practical_cheminformatics_tutorials/1-overview). The tutorials progress from fundamentals (RDKit, SMILES/SMARTS, molecular representation) through data manipulation with ChEMBL, clustering methods, structure-activity relationship analysis, and machine learning models for property prediction, through to generative models for de novo molecule design. Primarily uses RDKit, pandas, and scikit-learn. Notebooks are runnable on Google Colab or locally.
